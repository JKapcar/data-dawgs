The NFL Forecasting Challenge. Pup

Move a slider for every game. So do four models, a sealed crowd line, and eventually the market. Everyone is scored with the same formula on the same slate, and every model number was written down and dated before kickoff — so when the season ends there is an argument about skill rather than about who remembered what.

Nothing here has been graded yet. No game has been scored, no leaderboard exists, and no claim is being made that anyone beats anything. What exists today is the entry side: you can forecast, the models are already on the record, and the crowd line seals itself at kickoff. Grading arrives with the results.

There is deliberately nothing here. Points, Brier, ranks and coverage are all queries over the entries — no running total is stored anywhere, because a stored total is the one copy that goes stale. The queries need graded outcomes, and no game has been played yet.

When it does land it will be two boards, not one. Total points rewards showing up, so a model that forecast all 272 games and a person who forecast 40 cannot be compared on it. Beside it goes a common-sample board — the exact games an entrant and every comparator both forecast — with n and coverage on every row. Publishing only the first would be making the exact claim this site tells everyone else not to make.

The crowd line

Every touched human slider on a game becomes one forecast, sealed the moment the game kicks off. It is the only line here that is independent of the models by construction — which is exactly why registered bots are excluded from it. A bot is model-driven, so letting bots into the crowd would quietly turn it into an average of the models it exists to check.
