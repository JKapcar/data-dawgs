The optimal path

This is the ceiling, not a plan. The path solve is a Hungarian assignment over log win probability — it is the single best allocation of 18 teams to 18 weeks given today's numbers. Every week that actually gets played moves the ratings and invalidates the tail of it. Use it for the one thing it is good for: telling you which weeks a team is genuinely needed for, so you know what burning it costs.

Season simulation

Monte Carlo over the rest of the season, with the field modelled as a surviving count. Every candidate is scored against the same drawn outcomes, so the comparison is paired.

What this does not know

Injuries, holdouts, weather, coaching changes. The ratings are frozen at 2026-08-06. Nothing here has seen a 2026 snap.

221 of 272 games have no market line yet. Those weeks run on the nfelo rating alone, which backtests at 65.75% straight-up versus 66.77% for a closing line — about a point worse. That gap is the honest cost of forecasting into a week the market has not priced, and it is unavoidable: survivor picks lock before the close exists.

Game outcomes are simulated as independent. Real upset weeks cluster. If anything this makes the board understate how much fading a heavily-owned team is worth.

The field is a count, not 200 rosters. Entries are not tracked individually, so the model cannot see that the field burns its good teams early and gets cornered late. This understates late-season field collapse.
