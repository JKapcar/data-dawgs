2026 Draft Strategy

Synthesized from Establish The Run's 2026 Draft Kit Pro, captured 7/28/26. ETR updates daily — rankings below are a snapshot; the structural arguments age better than the player takes.

Read this first. Everything here mixes two kinds of claims. Structural claims (how positional scoring curves behave, where the dead zone sits) are testable across many seasons. Player-level calls are one analyst's priors — and a subscription product has an incentive to ship differentiated takes. Big Silva-vs-ADP deltas are part signal, part product.

The macro thesis: the RB dead zone is back

RB ADP is at a five-year high, which re-arms the classic inefficiency: in 12-team leagues, RBs drafted in Rounds 3–6 tend to underperform their cost while WRs in the same range outperform. The structural shape behind it: RB scoring falls off a cliff early and then runs nearly flat from about Round 3 through Round 10, while WR scoring stays flat through Rounds 5–6 and then drops fast.

The playbook that follows: secure one or two elite RBs early, or punt the position past the dead zone entirely. What you should not do is pay sticker price for mid-round RBs.

Anchor rules (12–14 team, half-PPR or fuller)

At least one RB in the first two rounds. At least three WRs by the end of Round 6. In 14+ team leagues the dead zone shifts up to Rounds 2–5. In 10-team-or-smaller leagues you get 8–9 rounds of WR runway — use it for elite QB/TE swings or early RB stacking, then backfill.

QB is a trap this year

The projection gap between the low-end QB1s and the mid-range QB2s is only about 10 total points across a season. The QB2 tier carries real rushing value (Dart ~8.5 rush PPG, Willis ~6.0, Murray ~4.7) plus efficiency outs like Purdy and Stafford. In sharp or larger redraft leagues: wait, and let someone else overdraft QB7–12.

One honest caveat ETR itself makes: the famous "wait forever on QB" structural edge from Allen/Lamar analysis is a best ball edge. It largely evaporates in one-QB redraft. And in small or casual leagues where opponents let value slip, paying up for Allen, Lamar, or Daniels is defensible — even correct.

TE mirrors QB

Bowers (major QB and play-caller upgrades), McBride (volume monster, with Carson Beck / offense-transition risk), and Loveland (ascending, Caleb's alpha target) head the position. Early TE swings are viable in small leagues and an opportunity-cost problem in big ones. Note: ETR's TE tiers file was still the 2025 version at capture — treat TE specifics as stale.

Build for Weeks 15–17, not Week 1

Championships are won in the fantasy playoffs, so the bench should tell a story: ascension archetypes late (rookies, contingent-value backups) mixed with floor veterans.

Week 17 stack targets

CHI–DET, JAX–WAS, BAL–CIN, LAR–TB, NYG–DAL.

Playoff schedules

Strong closes: MIN, ATL (WAS/TB/NO), ARI, and JAX/NYG after Week 15. Rough closes: PHI, SF, KC, HOU. Early-season notes: fade JAX/ARI in September (buy low late October); Stroud and Goff profile as elite September streamers; LAC/BAL/NYG open easy.

2026 storylines priced into the tiers

Chiefs went RB-centric (Walker paid, Mahomes coming off ACL/LCL, Rice suspension risk, Kelce at 37). The Saints are suddenly fantasy-relevant (fastest pace, indoors, Shough breakout profile, Etienne as the fulcrum). Vikings are a revival bet on Kyler. Dolphins read as toxic (Achane pricing, tempo questions, second-toughest schedule). Bengals are scoring-leader favorites. The Patriots added A.J. Brown for Maye.

Pace layer (Thorman)

Up in pace: CIN (via pass rate), DAL, DEN, IND, MIN, NO (fastest), TEN. Sloggers: BUF, LAC, LV, MIA, NYG, PIT, SF. League-wide, pace and pass rates keep sinking — motion, short passes, clock grind.

Format playbooks

Redraft vs. best ball

Better in redraft: contingent-upside lottery tickets you can cut when the contingency dies (Jordan James/Kaelon Black, Giddens/McGowan, MarShawn Lloyd, the Houston WR2 trio, Travis Hunter, Chig Okonkwo, Keaton Mitchell, Terrance Ferguson). Worse in redraft: spike-week specialists you can't time (Pierce, Watson, Tate, TeSlaa) and elite QBs.

Auction

Write a plan with target prices before the room opens. Nominate with an objective — drain budgets on players you don't want, and throw your actual targets out early before the room calibrates. Never price-enforce. Don't wait for the last player in a tier. Track budgets, spend to roughly zero, and budget the endgame.

Large leagues (14+)

RB/WR early and often, QB late, ride 3–4 week defense stretches instead of streaming weekly, spend FAAB early and hard, archetype-fit the bench.

Small leagues (≤10)

Elite QB/TE swings are viable, stream D/K freely, stay hyperactive on a loaded wire, and build max-upside benches.
