Bozo night mode — Midnight Midway

This file is the visual spec for the Bozo night mode. It is production-shaped CSS: open it in a browser, then lift values and the two ornaments out of it. Where this file and any prose description disagree, this file wins.

Three things this file gets deliberately wrong about the repo, because it is a standalone mockup and not the page:

1. It hard-codes hex values inline. In bozo.html these are the base :root playbill tokens — the site is dark-first, with :root[data-theme="light"] as the override. There is no [data-theme="dark"] selector on this site and there must not be one.

2. It paints the house-ticket slip dark. The repo deliberately keeps --slip-* paper in both themes ("a real receipt on a dark table"). Keep the repo's call: a lit paper ticket in a dark closed fairground is the stronger image anyway. Only the bill around it goes dark.

3. It draws its own bulb strip. bozo.html already has one — .machine::before, four repeating radial layers, driven by --bulb, --bulb-off, --bulbo, --bulbspeed. Reuse that system; do not add a second bulb implementation.

How the bozo is named

Only legs that lost are eligible — cash yours and you walk, however ugly the price was. Among the losers, the levers below decide, in an order that is drawn fresh every single week.

I

Shortest odds

The biggest favorite in the pool. The chalkiest leg on the ticket, if it lost, is the first one anybody looks at.

II

Worst beat

Finished furthest under its number, measured in standard deviations — so a blowout in a low-variance market counts for more than a blowout anywhere.

III

Last in

The final leg submitted. The only lever you can move by acting: it belongs to whoever files last, and it moves to the next person the moment they enter.

IV

Worst CLV

The price that moved most against it between submission and close. Not measured yet

Prices are self-reported. Every number on a board is typed in by the player who filed it. Nothing here checks it against DraftKings or any other book, and nothing here ever will vouch for one.

Bozo odds are simulation. Anywhere this site quotes a chance of wearing it, that is Monte Carlo output, not an observation, and the simulation draws every leg independently.

CLV is not measured yet. No closing prices are captured, so Worst CLV names nobody and no CLV figure on this site is real. The lever stays in the draw and falls through to the next one.

The draw is the server’s. The running order is one permutation, drawn at the moment the ticket locks and written once. Nothing in your browser can re-roll it.
