  <h1>Markets <span class="tierchip">Pup</span></h1>
  <p class="lead">This page measures prediction markets. <b>It does not sell picks.</b> Every price
  here is somebody else&rsquo;s forecast, and the only question we ask is whether it was any good.</p>
  <p class="sub">Prices come straight from Polymarket&rsquo;s public API in your browser &mdash; nothing is
  cached, transcribed or hand-typed on this page. The timestamp on each panel is the moment
  <em>your</em> browser fetched it, not the moment we built the page.</p>

  <div class="card" id="domains">
    <h3>Is it politics, or is it everything? <span class="rt" id="domAsOf"></span></h3>
    <p class="sub">The same markets as the calibration pass, split by what they are <em>about</em>.
    There is a live disagreement in the literature about which way prediction-market bias runs, and
    it may be that both sides are right about different subjects.</p>

    <div class="ddprereg">
      <h4>The claim being checked</h4>
      <p style="margin:6px 0"><b>One camp</b> (Le, arXiv:2602.19520, Aug 2026 — 64.7M Kalshi trades,
      cross-checked on 288.7M Polymarket trades) finds politics badly miscalibrated and everything
      else nearly clean, with prices <em>compressed toward 50%</em> — meaning long shots are
      <b>under</b>priced.</p>
      <p style="margin:6px 0"><b>The other camp</b> is the older, larger literature — and
      ElectionBettingOdds&rsquo; own 807-prediction record, where the 0&ndash;20% bucket predicted
      5.4% and delivered 1.7%. That is the textbook favourite&ndash;longshot bias, and it points
      the <em>opposite</em> way: long shots <b>over</b>priced.</p>
      <p style="margin:6px 0">Both cannot be right about the same markets. Different venues, eras
      and subjects are the obvious suspects. This panel is the cheapest honest test we can run.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="ddwarn"><b>Expect this panel to say &ldquo;does not separate them&rdquo; for a
    long time &mdash; and that is the correct answer, not a broken tool.</b> We tested the slope
    estimator against synthetic data with a known answer: it recovers the true slope to within
    ±0.05 at 4,000 markets, but at 150 markets the 95% interval is already ±0.29, and at 25 it is
    ±0.67. Telling a politics slope of 1.4 apart from a rest-of-market 1.0 needs intervals under
    about ±0.2 each &mdash; roughly 300 markets <em>per subject</em>. A single 60-market pass
    cannot get there. Run 240 and read the intervals, not the point estimates.</div>

    <div class="ddwarn" style="border-color:var(--ink-3);margin-top:16px">
      <b>Read the sample size before the slope.</b> Splitting a few hundred markets four ways leaves
      some buckets with a couple of dozen, and a slope fitted on 25 markets is nearly meaningless.
      Any bucket under 25 gets no slope at all rather than a decorative one. Subjects come from
      Polymarket&rsquo;s own tags, matched to buckets by a fixed keyword list &mdash; a market
      tagged both &ldquo;Politics&rdquo; and &ldquo;Crypto&rdquo; lands in whichever comes first in
      that list, and markets matching nothing land in <em>Other / untagged</em>, which is therefore a
      grab bag and not a clean category. Note that the headline compares politics against
      <em>all</em> non-political markets &mdash; a larger set than the Other / untagged row.
    </div>

  <div id="sheetMkMethod">
  <div class="card">
    <h3>Method, and what is missing</h3>
    <p class="sub">Written before the tools were interesting enough to be worth defending.</p>

    <p><b>What is live.</b> Two things, both computed in your browser from Polymarket&rsquo;s public
    API at the moment you load the page: the open-market board, and the calibration pass. Neither
    has a cached copy on this site, so neither can go stale &mdash; but neither is reproducible
    either, because you and I will get slightly different numbers. That is the trade we made to ship
    something true on day one instead of something frozen.</p>

    <p><b>What is not here yet, and why.</b></p>
    <ul>
      <li><b>Kalshi.</b> Their API sends no CORS header, so a browser cannot read it directly. It
      needs a proxy through our Worker. Until that exists, every number on this page is Polymarket
      only, and this page should not be read as a statement about prediction markets in general.</li>
      <li><b>The domain split</b> &mdash; politics vs. sports vs. crypto vs. weather. This is the
      interesting version of the calibration chart and it is the next thing built. There is a live
      disagreement in the literature about which direction the bias runs, and it looks resolvable
      with exactly the data this page already fetches.</li>
      <li><b>Cross-venue basis, parlay markup, volume-vs-accuracy.</b> Specced, not built.</li>
    </ul>

    <p><b>The calculators already on this site</b> &mdash; devig, expected value, hedging, odds
    conversion &mdash; are prediction-market tools that never had a live feed behind them.
    <a href="calculators.html">They are here.</a> Connecting them to this page is a
    build, not a research question.</p>

    <p class="sub"><b>Tier: Pup.</b> Live and useful, not yet validated. Nothing on this page has
    been checked against an independent implementation, and the calibration pass in particular has
    never been run against a known-answer test set. Treat every number as a first draft.</p>
