  <h1>AI <span class="tierchip">Pup</span></h1>
  <p class="lead">This page measures models. <b>It does not rank which one is best</b> &mdash; where
  the leaders are inside each other&rsquo;s error bars, the honest answer is &ldquo;tied,&rdquo; and
  that is what we will say.</p>
  <p class="sub">Everything on the first two panels is read live from OpenRouter&rsquo;s public model
  catalogue in your browser. Nothing is transcribed. The third panel is a study that has not
  produced a single data point yet, published in advance so that it cannot be quietly edited after
  the fact.</p>

  <!-- ===================== FORECAST STUDY ===================== -->
  <div id="sheetForecasts">
  <div class="card" id="forecasts">
    <h3>Does a better prompt make a better forecaster?</h3>
    <p class="lead" style="font-size:16px">We are about to find out, and we are telling you how
    before we start.</p>

    <div style="text-align:center;margin:22px 0 8px">
      <div class="ddcount" id="fcCount">0 / 150</div>
      <div class="sub" style="margin-top:2px">resolved questions &mdash; the pre-registered analysis
      does not run until the counter fills</div>
    </div>

    <p>Frontier models get the same set of live prediction-market questions, twice. Once
    <b>cold</b>, with no system prompt at all. Once with a fixed <b>standard reasoning prompt</b>
    &mdash; base rates, explicit priors, a stated posterior, an outside-view check. Same questions,
    same timestamp, same temperature, same output format. The system prompt is the only thing that
    differs.</p>

    <p>Then every forecast gets scored three ways: against what actually happened, against the
    market price at the moment the model saw the question, and against that price
    <em>recalibrated</em> using the curve on our own
    <a href="markets.html#calibration">Markets page</a>. That third one is the interesting test. If a
    model only beats the market by the amount the market&rsquo;s known miscalibration already
    explains, it did not find anything &mdash; it rediscovered a bias we had already published.</p>

    <div class="ddprereg">
      <h4>The three rules that keep it honest</h4>
      <p style="margin:6px 0"><b>1. No tools, no search, either arm.</b> A model with web access
      reads the market price instead of forecasting, and a prompt that <em>tells</em> a model to go
      look things up would make the two arms differ in information rather than in reasoning. Turning
      tools off is what keeps this a test of the prompt.</p>
      <p style="margin:6px 0"><b>2. The same forced output line in both arms.</b> A reasoning prompt
      asks for ranges and posteriors; scoring needs one number. Without an identical output
      contract, the disciplined arm gets punished for following its own instructions and we would be
      measuring our parser.</p>
      <p style="margin:6px 0"><b>3. Forward-looking questions only.</b> Every question resolves after
      the forecast date and after the model&rsquo;s training cutoff. A model recalling an outcome is
      not forecasting it.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="ddprereg">
      <h4>Written down before any model was run</h4>
      <p style="margin:6px 0">Probability the disciplined prompt wins at all: <b>55–70%</b>.
      That it wins by more than 0.02 Brier: <b>20–35%</b>. That it is actively
      <em>worse</em>: <b>20–30%</b> &mdash; long reasoning chains can talk a model out of a
      well-anchored prior. That any model beats the recalibrated market: <b>under 20%</b>, and we
      would treat such a result as a bug until a second cohort reproduced it.</p>
      <p style="margin:6px 0"><b>What would change our minds about the whole design:</b> if the cold
      arm already produces long, structured, base-rate-citing reasoning without being asked, then
      the two arms are nearly the same thing and a null result means &ldquo;the prompt was
      redundant,&rdquo; not &ldquo;prompting does not work.&rdquo; That gets checked on the first ten
      questions, before scaling.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="ddwarn"><b>The limitation we cannot design away yet.</b> This compares one prompt
    against <em>no</em> prompt. It cannot separate &ldquo;this specific reasoning framework&rdquo;
    from &ldquo;having any long system prompt at all&rdquo; &mdash; that needs a third,
    placebo arm of similar length, and it is not running. Until it does, no claim on this page
    should be read as one prompt beating another.</p></div>

    <p class="sub"><b>Status: pre-registration, not results.</b> Nothing has been run. When the first
    cohort is collected the raw model outputs get published alongside the scores &mdash; the
    reasoning bodies, verbatim, including whatever they hallucinate.</p>
  </div>
  </div>

  <!-- ===================== METHOD ===================== -->
  <div id="sheetAiMethod">
  <div class="card">
    <h3>Method, and what is missing</h3>

    <p><b>What is live.</b> The model board and the frontier chart, both computed in your browser
    from one OpenRouter request at page load. No cached mirror exists under <code>/data/</code> for
    this page yet, which cuts both ways honestly: the numbers cannot go stale, and they are not
    reproducible &mdash; you and I will see slightly different figures depending on when we looked.</p>

    <p><b>What is deliberately not here.</b></p>
    <ul>
      <li><b>A tie-aware leaderboard.</b> This was specced and then cut, because the intelligence
      index in OpenRouter&rsquo;s payload arrives with no confidence interval. Collapsing
      statistically indistinguishable models into one block is the whole point of the tool, and
      inventing an interval to do it would be worse than not shipping it. It needs LMArena&rsquo;s
      published Bradley-Terry bounds, which are a separate fetch.</li>
      <li><b>Epoch AI benchmark data.</b> Their CSVs are CC-BY and current, but the domain sends no
      CORS header, so a browser cannot read them directly. Needs a Worker proxy.</li>
      <li><b>METR&rsquo;s time-horizon revision audit.</b> The most interesting chart we have
      specced &mdash; how much of the apparent acceleration is models improving versus the measuring
      instrument being changed underneath them. It is gated: it does not ship until the logistic
      fits are recomputed from METR&rsquo;s public repository rather than read off their blog post.
      Publishing an accusation of a measurement artifact on the strength of secondary numbers would
      be the exact failure this site exists to avoid.</li>
    </ul>

    <p class="sub"><b>Tier: Pup.</b> Live and useful, not validated. The board is a faithful
    rendering of somebody else&rsquo;s catalogue &mdash; if OpenRouter&rsquo;s prices are wrong,
    ours are wrong in exactly the same way, and we have not independently checked a single one
    against a provider&rsquo;s own pricing page.</p>
  </div>
