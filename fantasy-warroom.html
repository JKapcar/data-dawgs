Method & footnotes

Inputs
Rosters, scoring and schedule come from your league provider at the moment you connect, and are frozen there. Projections are Sleeper's, third-party and unaudited; this page produces none of its own. ⚠️ Every simulated week uses the same week-1 projection. No opponent adjustment, no bye, no rest-of-season curve — a season is modelled as fourteen copies of week 1 plus noise. That is the single largest gap between what the odds look like and what they are worth.
Replacement level
Every team's starting lineup is filled from the projectable pool — dedicated slots first, then each flex slot to the best remaining eligible player. The best player nobody started is the replacement at his position, and every starter is priced against that. Flex demand therefore lands where the players actually land instead of being allocated by a positional share.
The simulation
20,000 seasons. Each week every team scores its VOR total plus a normal draw with standard deviation σ. The seed is derived from the league ID and your settings, so identical inputs give an identical number every time — rerun it and check. A sanity check asserts that expected berths sum to the playoff spots, and throws rather than reporting a number if they don't.
What a trade proposal has to clear
Three gates. Both starting lineups must improve when the slot assignment is re-run after the swap. A swap that would leave either side unable to fill a starting slot it could fill before is refused outright, whatever the points say — that is how you get offered your only quarterback for a wideout. And the two players must be within max($4, 35% of the dearer player) of each other in Public Market Value — the auction dollars in /data/pool.json, maintained by hand each week, in whichever column matches this league (full PPR, half, or superflex). If either player has no published price, that pairing falls back to max(1.0, 35%) in projected value over replacement and the row is labelled as model-based.
Why market value and not points
A bench player contributes exactly zero to a starting-lineup total. Judge a trade on lineup gain alone and a rival holding a benched WR1 "gains" by shipping him for anything that fills an empty slot — which is how this tool once offered Harrison Butker for Jaylen Waddle and reported both lineups improving. It did. The arithmetic was right and the question was wrong. Market value asks the question a league-mate actually answers. Prices are a dated snapshot, not a live market, and every board says which date it is using. This is a candidate screen, not a fairness oracle: it cannot see acceptance probability, a manager's preferences, depth needs, injury risk, or multi-player construction.
Which value horizon is used
Redraft and keeper leagues use the dated this-season market snapshot in /data/pool.json. Sleeper dynasty leagues expose a toggle between that this-season price and the independent overall board in /data/dynasty-ranks.json, selecting 1QB or Superflex / TE-premium dynasty values from the actual roster slots. The Money sheet shows both prices and their delta for every player. Keeper rules vary too much to infer a multi-year value without contract length and keeper cost, so the page labels them and stays on this-season values.
Not modeled
Injuries, byes, trades, waivers, lineup decisions, in-season projection updates, and correlation between the two teams in a matchup. Before three completed weeks, σ is a stated generic assumption, not an estimate from your league.
Not built yet
Rest-of-season or per-week projections · ESPN and Yahoo authenticated reads · the man-games replacement baseline · multi-player and 2-for-1 trades · playoff-odds re-simulation of a proposed trade.
Receipts
This tool issues none. Its odds are recomputed live from current inputs and are never logged before kickoff, so they are not receipts and do not appear on the scoreboard. Treat them as a calculator, not a prediction record. The graded work is on Receipts.
