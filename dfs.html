Step 1 — Solver

Builds the highest-projected lineup

An exact solve against the salary cap, subject to exposure caps, uniques, stack rules and bring-back. It answers what scores most.

→
Step 2 — Simulator

Ranks those lineups by how often they win

Generates a field that mirrors projected ownership, plays the slate thousands of times, and sorts by ROI. It answers what beats the room.

These are different questions. The highest-projected lineup is frequently a bad tournament entry: if everyone else builds it too, the payout splits. Build a pool with the Solver, then pick your entries with the Simulator.

Modules

01

Slate & paste-in projections

Site, game type and slate. Salary CSV plus your own projections — the tool never pretends to have a better number than you do.

02

Ownership & leverage

Ownership is a separate data layer, per contest. Everything game-theoretic downstream is only as good as this estimate.

03

Solver settings

Lock / exclude, min-max salary, per-player and positional exposure caps, uniques between lineups, stack type and bring-back.

04

Exact solver

A real integer program, not a greedy fill. MIT-licensed and running in the browser — no lineup-count paywall.

05

Three-factor contest sim

Game environment × team share × individual variance, then a generated field. Outputs ROI, cash rate, win rate and duplication risk.

06

Exposure & portfolio

Running exposure by player and by team stack, so a 150-lineup pool is a portfolio rather than 150 copies of one opinion.

07

DraftKings CSV export

Upload-ready in the site's own format.

What this can't do

These are projections, not promises — every point total is a median on a distribution, and the sim only shows the range of finishes given our correlation assumptions. It cannot know late injury news, weather after lock, or the true shape of the field. Ownership is an estimate, not DraftKings' actuals, and leverage is only ever as good as the ownership guess underneath it. Paid incumbents run $97–$297 a month and gate the simulator behind lineup-count caps; this being free is not evidence that it is better.
