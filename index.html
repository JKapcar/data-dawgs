Data Dawgs — Data, Not Dogma

A digital sandbox for data and ideas. Ruthlessly truth-seeking.

What this is

Data Dawgs is a playground. Ideas come in as hunches — a draft strategy, a market that looks mispriced, a claim somebody repeated with a lot of confidence — and they get pointed at real numbers to see which ones survive contact. Some do. Most don't. That part is the fun.

Nothing here is selling a system. The tools get built to answer a question honestly, including when the honest answer is we don't know yet, or less than you'd think, or that edge was gone three seasons ago.

Data, not dogma

Dogma is a belief that stopped paying rent. It got repeated because it was true once, or because someone confident said it, or because everyone in the room already agreed. At some point it quit being tested and started being defended.

Data is what you test it against. Not because numbers are sacred — they're noisy, they're collected by people with incentives, and a bad number held confidently is worse than no number at all. But a number can be checked. A number can be wrong in a way you're able to see. Dogma can't.

So the rule is that evidence wins. Strong claims get an outside-view check first: what's the base rate for this class of thing, and across how many seasons? Point estimates get treated as ranges, because that's what they are. Everything carries a date, because everything decays. And when the argument is for a conclusion we already liked, the actual job is catching ourselves selecting the evidence.

Being wrong early and quietly is cheap. Being wrong late and loudly is what costs you.

What's here, and what's coming

Today it's built out for fantasy football: an EPA explorer running on public play-by-play, a 2026 draft thesis with its sources and its expiration date attached, and a live auction room that runs a 14-team draft off a laptop and a projector.

That's the first tenant, not the blueprint. Next through the door: prediction markets, forecasting and calibration tracking, sports betting models, game theory, and AI — anything where you can state a claim, write down a number, and find out later whether you were right.
