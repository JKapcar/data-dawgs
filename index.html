Data, not dogma

Dogma is a belief that stopped paying rent. It got repeated because it was true once, or because someone confident said it, or because everyone in the room already agreed — and somewhere in there it quit being tested and started being defended.

Data is what you test it against. Not because numbers are sacred; they're noisy, they're collected by people with incentives, and a bad number held confidently is worse than none at all. But a number can be checked. A number can be wrong in a way you're able to see. Dogma can't.

So this is a sandbox. Ideas come in as hunches, get pointed at real numbers, and most don't survive. Strong claims get an outside-view check first — what's the base rate, across how many seasons? Estimates are ranges, not points. Everything carries a date. The honest answer is often we don't know yet, or less than you'd think. And when the argument is for a conclusion we already liked, the job is catching ourselves selecting the evidence.

Being wrong early and quietly is cheap. Being wrong late and loudly is what costs you.

What's here, and what's coming

Today it's built out for fantasy football: an EPA explorer running on public play-by-play, a 2026 draft thesis with its sources and its expiration date attached, and a live auction room that runs a 14-team draft off a laptop and a projector.

That's the first tenant, not the blueprint. Next through the door: prediction markets, forecasting and calibration tracking, sports betting models, game theory, and AI — anything where you can state a claim, write down a number, and find out later whether you were right.
