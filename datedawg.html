DATEDAWG
Reads a Hinge export · nothing leaves this page

Of the women you liked, how many liked you back — and where does that rank?

That is reciprocal acceptance — she saw you in her likes queue, already knowing you liked her. It is not the odds a stranger likes you cold in discover; that has no denominator in the export and this page will not pretend otherwise. What the file does hold is every like you sent and whether it landed, which is enough to rank against published distributions of the same measure.

This page never uploads anything. To check rather than trust: turn off your network and load your export anyway — it still works. The verification selfie file is never opened, and your email, phone, device IPs and photo links are discarded as the file is read.

Drop your export files here

matches.json is the only one required. Adding user.json shows your filters.

Optional advanced test — declare a change date before loading (and label the subject)

Only use this if one real-world date already matters — for example, new photos went live or you moved city. A date chosen after a dashboard is on screen is a date you chose knowing the answer. That is a search result, not a finding, so DateDawg will not put a p-value on it. Otherwise, leave this closed and use the dashboard's date filters. There is no way to declare a test after loading the export.

DateDawg is part of the Dawgs section of datadawgs216.com. Every figure shows its numerator and denominator. Rankings are derived from published distribution parameters and shown as a spread across assumptions rather than a single number, with the assumptions printed so you can argue with them. Timestamps are read as UTC.
